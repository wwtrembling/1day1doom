A grizzled ARTIST clone, smeared with faded oil paint, hunches over a scavenged canvas. His once-stylish beret is now a crudely patched rag, stained with what might be clone-blood or cheap synthetic pigments. He clutches a half-melted paintbrush, the bristles matted, and wears spectacles with one cracked lens. His face is a mask of vacant boredom, punctuated by a twitching eye. The "art" he’s creating is a pathetic, repeated drawing of a can of generic soup, meticulously rendered, yet utterly devoid of emotion. Behind him, the crumbling remnants of a gallery display the horrifying truth: identical, lifeless clones of famous artworks are peeling off the walls, replaced by crude, desperate scribbles of "I am." The background reveals a street filled with abandoned, identical clones staring blankly at each other, mirroring the artist's existential dread.
