A frazzled office worker clone, "Kevin," hunches over a flickering terminal in a derelict cubicle farm. Kevin’s beige, ill-fitting suit is ripped at the seams, held together with duct tape fashioned from what appears to be a company-branded sticky note dispenser. His tie, once a symbol of corporate conformity, is now a makeshift tourniquet for a crudely bandaged arm – likely the result of a skirmish with an identity-theft-obsessed clone. His expression is a wide-eyed, vacant stare, perpetually stuck between bewilderment and existential dread. He frantically clicks the mouse, trying to delete a spreadsheet titled “Reasons We Are Not Real,” dodging the occasional erratic swipe from a rogue, discarded stapler. The background is a chaotic tapestry of overturned water coolers, broken monitors displaying repeating error messages, and the scattered remains of motivational posters, now defaced with frantic scrawls of "WHO AM I?"
