A sanitation worker clone, "Unit 734," shuffles down a flooded, neon-slicked alley. His orange jumpsuit is ripped, held together with duct tape and scavenged wires; a biohazard symbol is crudely spray-painted over his chest. A dented, rusty garbage can lid serves as a makeshift shield. His face, identical to countless others, is a mask of bewildered terror, eyes darting from side to side. He clutches a repurposed, broken plunger, now his only weapon. Unit 734 cautiously navigates a sea of discarded clone-body parts – limbs, torsos, and vaguely familiar, vacant faces. The putrid smell of synthetic flesh and overflowing bio-waste hangs heavy in the air. In the background, a flickering holographic advertisement for "Authentic Human Experiences" glitches erratically, mocking his reality.
