A student clone, clutching a tattered textbook titled "Cloning for Dummies," rummages through a trash heap of discarded "original" clothes. Their ill-fitting, slightly-too-large lab coat, stained with what’s hopefully just cafeteria food, has been crudely patched with duct tape and ripped jeans. A pair of mismatched, thick-rimmed glasses are taped together at the bridge, perpetually sliding down their nose, revealing a perpetually bewildered expression. Their once-perfectly-cloned face is now smeared with grime, punctuated by a single, defiant smudge of bright pink lipstick stolen from a discarded cosmetics kit. The clone desperately tries to identify a crumpled photo of a cat amidst the junk, muttering, "Was… was this me? Am I the cat person?". Towering above, the decaying, vacant faces of replaced humans, all identical, stare blankly from the windows of a partially collapsed university building, their own lab coats flapping in the wind.
