A frazzled Accountant, his once-pristine suit now patched with duct tape and covered in what suspiciously looks like coffee stains, hunches over a flickering laptop salvaged from a dumpster. His tie, a garish neon green, is knotted haphazardly around his neck. His face is a mask of weary desperation, eyes bloodshot and darting nervously. He's surrounded by a chaotic pile of shredded tax documents and a half-eaten bag of cloned-human-flavored nutrient paste.

He frantically clicks the mouse, desperately trying to access a secure server amidst the digital chaos. Scattered around him are discarded "authentic human" identification cards, each with a different, panicked expression photo. In the background, a horde of identical clones – each blank-faced and wearing ill-fitting, mismatched clothes – shuffle slowly through a dilapidated office building, their collective drone of monotone conversation the soundtrack to his lonely, accounting-based apocalypse.
