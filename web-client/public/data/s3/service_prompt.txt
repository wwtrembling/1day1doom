A cloned barista, “Brenda,” hunches in a skeletal Starbucks. Her tattered apron, once crisp, is now patched with duct tape and emblazoned with a crudely drawn skull. A chipped plastic name tag, reading "Brenda," is askew on her chest. Brenda's eyes are wide, unfocused – pure existential dread. She nervously clutches a rusty, repurposed espresso machine, scavenging for grounds. One hand absentmindedly fiddles with a tangle of wires that now replaces her cloned arm. Scattered around her are half-melted, sentient coffee cups, begging for refills in monotone voices. The background shows a chaotic streetscape, populated by identical, blank-faced clones aimlessly wandering, occasionally bumping into each other. A flickering neon sign in the distance reads “Cloned Coffee: We’re All the Same (Maybe?).”
